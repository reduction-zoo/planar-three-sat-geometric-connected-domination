## Evidence and diagnosis

The two fixed [sources](negative_cases.json) are legal planar incidence embeddings. Their clauses, after duplicate removal, each have two distinct noncomplementary literals, so unit propagation has no initial unit. Exhaustive truth tables in [test_negative_sources.py](test_negative_sources.py) establish UNSAT independently: the four-sign-pair formula excludes all four assignments to two variables; the opposed-pair-cycle formula forces its first variable both ways. F under hash seeds 7 and 99 emits identical legal nontrivial targets: 31,576 points with K=16,401 and 25,236 points with K=13,231. These cases remain outside the original fixed 112-case Prepare corpus.

The [target-only certificate checker](certify_negative.py) executes public F as a subprocess and reads only its explicit integer points and K for the negative decision. For distinct integer points, unit Euclidean adjacency is exactly one horizontal or vertical grid step. The checker reconstructs the full point graph, partitions it into branch vertices, disjoint paths and one side point for every nonconnector interior path vertex, and checks every main and side edge against this structure. It obtains a 612-vertex, 864-edge skeleton in each case and derives connected-cover budget B=308 from K and the checked path counts. This is the exact structural interface of Clark, Colbourn and Johnson, [*Unit disk graphs* (1990), Theorem 6.1 and Lemmas 6.1–6.2](https://cs.du.edu/~snarayan/sada/research/docs/res/unitdisk.pdf): a target CDS of size at most K would give a connected vertex cover of the skeleton of size at most B. The certificate is therefore conditional on that published normalization/counting lemma; it is not a generic direct CDS solver.

Every connected vertex cover of a connected graph contains its articulation vertices. The checker finds 240 in each skeleton. It then applies 57 exact degree-two vertex-cover folds to the graph induced by the remaining vertices. A fold removes a degree-two vertex v with nonadjacent neighbors a,b and introduces z adjacent to their external neighbors. An optimal original cover can be normalized to choose v or both a,b: if it chooses v and just one neighbor, replace v by the other neighbor at equal cost. Selecting z in the folded graph represents a,b, while omitting z represents v, so each fold lowers the optimum by exactly one. The residual graph has 18 vertices; exhaustive enumeration gives minimum vertex cover 12 in both cases. Thus every connected cover has at least 240+57+12=309 vertices, exceeding B=308. [Machine-readable certificates](certificates.json) retain target digests, all counts and the 18-vertex residual edge lists. G was separately run on the certified `NO-SOLUTION` output and returned `NO-SOLUTION` for both sources.

[Fold checks](test_certificate.py) matched independent exact vertex-cover enumeration on all 209 graph-atlas graphs through six vertices. The same parser and lower-bound code accepted prepared positive case 1 as a control, yielding a lower bound within its budget. Both source and certificate scripts passed with `uv run --locked python campaigns/planar-three-sat-geometric-connected-domination/rounds/041/<script>.py` for `test_negative_sources`, `test_certificate` and `certify_negative`.

The generic separator heuristic measured widths 16 and 14 but did not deliver a direct CDS answer in this experiment; a raw cover SMT attempt was inconclusive/interrupted and is not used as NO evidence. The structural certificates rely on the published implication and on explicit target-only graph checks. They add two nontrivial negative instances, not an unrestricted negative-target solver.
